I · Market Growth

Published estimates of the handmade soap market's size diverge by an order of magnitude, because firms define "handmade" differently. The growth rate does not diverge — every source lands between 6% and 7.5%. So use the growth rate and ignore the levels.

II · Why Soap, Physically

The corn analysis found Kenyon's core product structurally hard to sell at a distance: perishable, heavy relative to value, seasonal. Soap is the same brand asset with inverted physics.

IV · The Freight Fix ← the important one

Cornmeal is dense, cheap and shipping-hostile. Soap is light and dear. Parcel cost rises with weight far more slowly than soap's retail value does, so every bar added improves the margin on the entire order.

VI · The Tallow Signal

But read the caveat. Trends decay, and this one is riding social media rather than a structural shift like the plastic-free move behind bar soap generally. Treat tallow as a reason to launch the heritage bar now rather than in two years — not as the foundation of the business. Also note: a tallow balm is a cosmetic, not soap, so full cosmetic rules attach. A tallow soap sold for cleansing does not.

VIII · Sequencing

Three things to do, in order.

1. Build gift boxes from what you already make. Weeks of work, immediate lift in average order value, and it creates the channel soap will occupy.

2. Set the free-shipping threshold just above average order value. One number. Converts Figure 4 into revenue, measurable within a month.

3. Run a 500–1,000 bar private-label soap trial using Kenyon's own cornmeal, mill store and DTC attach only. Measure attach rate and reorder rate before committing to anything larger.

And one thing not to do: do not make skin-benefit claims. True soap sold solely for cleansing sits outside FDA cosmetic regulation and MoCRA entirely. That exemption is worth more than any marketing copy it would buy — and the provenance story you should tell instead is the better story anyway.
