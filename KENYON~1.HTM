How this is calibrated. The fair-value estimate uses the reciprocal stocks-to-use specification Price = a + b(1/SUR) standard in the agricultural economics literature, fitted by ordinary least squares to marketing years 2013–2026 (n = 14): a = 1.31, b = 0.358, R² ≈ 0.40, σ ≈ 0.80. Forty percent of the variance — respectable for a single-variable model of a commodity price, and nowhere near enough to bet the business on. Re-estimate on the full ERS history (1975–present, ~50 rows) with corn_price_model.R and update A, B and SIGMA at the top of the script block below.



Revision, 25 September 2026. Running the September WASDE through the original logic exposed a flaw: with stocks-to-use at 9.7% and December futures at the 79th percentile, the gauge returned WAIT. That was wrong. When stocks are tight and falling, the price distribution is skewed sharply upward and the cost of being caught short exceeds the cost of paying up — percentile ranking alone cannot see that. The gauge now takes prior-month stocks, reports the direction of travel and the marginal sensitivity ($/bu per point of SUR), and suppresses a WAIT signal whenever stocks are tightening.

What this cannot do. It does not forecast specialty flint corn prices — no public series exists, and flint is only loosely tethered to Chicago. It will not beat the futures market. Use it to know whether the market is tight or loose, and to turn a corn price into the two numbers you actually manage: cost per pound, and months of supply on hand.
